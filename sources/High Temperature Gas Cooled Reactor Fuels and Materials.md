# High Temperature Gas Cooled Reactor Fuels and Materials

> IAEA, Vienna, 2010. IAEA-TECDOC-1645-CD. ISBN 978-92-0-153110-2. Printed March 2010 (p.4).
> [PDF](../raw/pdf/high-temp-gas-cooled-reactors-fuels.pdf) · [converted](../raw/md/high-temp-gas-cooled-reactors-fuels/high-temp-gas-cooled-reactors-fuels.md) · [chapters](../raw/md/high-temp-gas-cooled-reactors-fuels/chapters/)

**Gist** — An IAEA handbook consolidating roughly fifty years of international coated-particle fuel research and development, written explicitly for the education and training of a new generation of engineers rather than as original research (p.5). It covers TRISO kernel and particle manufacture, spherical fuel and compact fabrication, QA/QC and characterization, in-core structural materials, irradiation testing, failure mechanisms, accident testing, fuel chemistry, fission product transport modelling, fuel cycles and spent fuel management, with an annex on nuclear hydrogen production. Its value is as a consolidated record of what has been demonstrated experimentally — above all the German AVR/FZJ (Forschungszentrum Jülich) and US Fort St. Vrain/NPR campaigns — not as a design document for any specific reactor.

## Character of the source

- **Compilation, not primary research.** Findings are drawn from cited programme reports; the handbook rarely presents new data of its own. Claims should be traced to the underlying references where precision matters.
- **Evidence base is German and US, and mostly pre-2009.** The newest results cited are from around 2008; the AVR and FZJ heating-test campaigns date from 1985–1994.
- **Spherical fuel elements dominate the test data**, even where conclusions are stated generally. Prismatic-specific behaviour is usually inferred by argument from power density and thermal gradient rather than measured directly — a distinction that matters for a prismatic design.
- Drafted by an international author group chaired by H. Nabielek (Germany), reviewed at consultancy meetings in 2006–2008 (p.7).

## Defects in the source document

> [!warning] Printing error in Table 10 (p.87)
> The AVR 74/10 row gives the ⁸⁵Kr fractional release at 1800 °C as `81.2E-3`. Verified against the PDF: **the typo is in the published document**, not in conversion. Neighbouring 1800 °C values are 1.7E-3 (AVR 70/33) and 6.5E-4 (HFR-K3/3), so the intended value is most likely 1.2E-3. Do not cite this number without flagging it.
> `#conflict`

> [!warning] Table numbering in the body text is unreliable
> Table captions form a consistent sequence across the document, but in-text references do not match them in chapters 4 and 5. Ch 4 calls the tables captioned **3** and **4** "Tables 4 and 5", the one captioned **5** "Table 6", and the one captioned **6** "Table 7". Ch 5 twice calls the table captioned **7** "Table 3", and directs the reader to "Chapter 8" for TRISO irradiations when that is Chapter 7. **Cite tables by caption number.**
> `#conflict`

Separately, marker's conversion of Table 10 misaligned several rows (AVR 85/18, AVR 90/2, AVR 90/20, AVR 74/11). All values used in [[Ch 09 — Accident Testing]] were read from the PDF pages directly, not from the converted markdown.
